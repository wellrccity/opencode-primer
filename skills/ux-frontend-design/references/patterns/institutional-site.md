# Institutional Site

Use when: building or reviewing an organization's public site: services,
institutional identity, trust content, contact, and published resources.

## Layout and hierarchy

- Top-level sections stay few and stable: for example organization, services,
  work or resources, contact. Keep the set small enough to scan in one glance;
  chunk or prioritize by visitor task rather than hitting a fixed number. The
  test is whether a visitor can name the right section after one glance.
- URL structure mirrors the section structure and does not change with
  redesigns. Slug changes ship with a permanent redirect map.
- Home page answers three questions in order: who this organization is, what
  it does for whom, and how to reach a human.
- Every service page carries: outcome for the client, what is included, who
  delivers it, proof, and one contact action.
- Team page lists real names, roles, and a route to contact. No stock
  portraits standing in for staff.
- Contact block provides a physical address, business hours, a phone number,
  and an email, plus a form as an alternative, not the only channel.
- News or resources use a dated archive with stable URLs; undated evergreen
  content belongs in a section page, not the news feed.

## Required states

- Default, hover, focus-visible, and current-section state in navigation.
- Search results: loading, no matches, and matches with a count.
- Contact form: default, validating, submitting, success, and failure with a
  fallback channel next to the error.
- News archive: empty month, paginated end, and a filtered no-match state.
- Resource download: unavailable file, expired resource, and access-denied.
- Long-content pages need a table of contents state for the active section.
- Localized pages indicate the selected language and the available others.

## Interaction rules

- Current section is marked with `aria-current="page"` and a visual state,
  not by color alone.
- Language switching keeps the visitor on the equivalent page where one
  exists; otherwise land on the localized home page and say so.
- Navigation depth stays shallow: any content page reachable within a few
  clicks from home. Keep a visible breadcrumb trail below the top level.
- Contact and quote forms ask only what the team will act on, and state the
  response time the organization actually honors.
- External document links state format and size ("PDF, 1.2 MB") so the
  visitor can decide before downloading.
- Anchors offset for a sticky header so headings are not hidden on arrival.
- No modal opens automatically on load; consent banners stay out of the
  primary reading path.

## Data and edge cases

- Content model: typed blocks (text, figure, quote, embed, contact) instead of
  free-form HTML pages, so editors cannot break layout or accessibility.
- Required CMS fields: title, slug, summary, social image, updated date,
  alt text for every uploaded image.
- Legal and trust data has an owner and a review date: registration number,
  address, certifications with issuing body and validity, privacy policy.
- Images come with a credit and a license field; replace, do not reuse, a
  photo whose license expired.
- Office hours and holidays are data, not prose inside an image.
- Redirect table for every retired URL, reviewed before launch; 404 pages
  offer search plus the main sections.
- Localization: translated routes or a stable canonical plus `hreflang`
  pairs; never hide text inside images.
- CMS constraint: editors publish drafts, preview at mobile width, and cannot
  upload an image above the agreed size budget without a warning.

## Accessibility

- One `h1` per page; section headings descend in order and are generated by
  the content blocks, not by font size choices.
- Landmarks: `header`, `nav` with an accessible name, `main`, `footer`; a skip
  link is the first focusable element.
- Every content page is operable by keyboard, including carousels, accordions,
  and embedded maps.
- Brand palettes frequently fail contrast; provide an accessible text variant
  and verify 4.5:1 for body text and 3:1 for large text and UI borders
  (WCAG 2.2 1.4.3, 1.4.11).
- Focus visible on all links and controls, not removed by a global reset
  (2.4.7).
- Uploaded PDFs are either accessible or accompanied by an HTML summary.
- Forms have labels, field-level errors, and a summary; errors are not
  color-only.
- Target size at least 24x24 CSS px (2.5.8); 44x44 recommended for mobile
  contact actions.
- Reflow at 320px and 200 percent zoom without horizontal scrolling (1.4.10).

## Performance

- Content pages are static or statically generated; no client framework is
  required to read text.
- Image budget agreed per template; serve responsive `srcset` sizes and
  modern formats, with explicit width and height to avoid layout shift.
- Fonts: subset, at most two families, `font-display: swap`; system fonts are
  an acceptable choice for low-end audiences.
- Third-party embeds (maps, video, chat) load on interaction behind a
  lightweight facade.
- Test on a throttled mid-range mobile profile and record the measurement;
  treat lab numbers as project data, not vendor claims.
- Core Web Vitals targets: LCP 2.5 s or less, INP 200 ms or less, CLS 0.1 or
  less at the 75th percentile.
- Analytics deferred and audited; no tag manager blocking first render.

## Anti-patterns

- A homepage carousel of unrelated messages competing for the same attention.
- Navigation that mixes services, campaigns, and internal departments at one
  level.
- Stock-photo hero images that say nothing about the organization.
- Contact hidden behind a form with no phone, email, or address.
- Undated news, expired certifications, and staff pages for people who left.
- Text baked into images, which breaks translation, search, and screen
  readers.
- Auto-playing video with sound, or a cookie wall covering the content.
- Invented awards, client logos without permission, or counters with no
  source.
- A CMS that lets editors paste styled HTML from a word processor.

## Checklist

- [ ] Top-level navigation labels map one-to-one to sections and to URLs.
- [ ] Visitor can reach contact details from every page in one action.
- [ ] Contact page lists address, hours, phone, email, and a form.
- [ ] Service pages state outcome, inclusions, owner, proof, and contact
      action.
- [ ] Trust claims (registration, certifications) carry a source and a date.
- [ ] News items show a publication date and have stable, shareable URLs.
- [ ] Images in the CMS require alt text before publish.
- [ ] Every retired URL in the redirect map returns a single permanent
      redirect, verified.
- [ ] Keyboard-only pass reaches main content, navigation, and the contact
      form.
- [ ] Contrast measured for brand colors in every text role; failing pairs
      have an accessible variant.
- [ ] 320px and 200 percent zoom: no horizontal scroll, no clipped headings.
- [ ] Throttled mobile test recorded for LCP, INP, and CLS on home and one
      service page.
- [ ] Language switch lands on the equivalent page or explains the fallback.
